My name is Karina Bellavia and I am a 2nd year computational biology Ph.D. student at Brown University. I am advised by [Dr. Katie Siddle](https://vivo.brown.edu/display/ksiddle). My research focuses on understanding how viruses evolve and emerge. I am currently studying Lassa fever, a hemorrhagic virus endemic to Western Africa. Humans are primarily infected through contact with the urine/feces of infected *Mastomys* rodents. I am interested in detecting genome-wide signatures of positive selection, as well as lineages and mutations with increased genetic fitness.

Prior to my Ph.D., I obtained my B.S. in Biology and minors in Mathematics & Public Health at St. Lawrence University in Canton, NY. While there, I was involved in insect behavior research, and did work to better understand the ethology and phenology of the robber fly species *Lasiopogon currani*. 

Towards the end of my bachelor's degree, I became interested in the intersections of mathematics, vector-borne diseases, and public health; thus, I decided to pursue my master's in Biostatistics at Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health. I was lucky to be mentored by Dr. Wesley Wong of [Dr. Dyann Wirth's](https://hsph.harvard.edu/research/wirth-lab/), where I leveraged population genetic variation to understand the evolution and transmission of malaria (*Plasmodium falciparum*).

I am passionate about the importance and value of education, and spend my free time tutoring at local schools in Providence. I also have countless hours of experience tutoring and being a teaching assistant for several courses. Beyond this, I am always working towards making STEM an inclusive and diverse space. As a first-generation student, I know first-hand how difficult and isolating it can feel to navigate graduate school and academia. I could not be where I am today without the mentors I have had, and to give back, I have held plenty of mentorship positions to help fellow first-generation students find their footing in undergraduate and graduate school. 

After my Ph.D., I plan to continue on the path to academia and hope to hold a professorship at a liberal arts college where I can teach and excite the next generation of scientists! :)
