**Introduction**

In malaria research, genomics is a powerful tool that uses genetic metrics to survey parasitic transmission such as the frequency of multiple strain infections, genetic clones, and the complexity of infection. Current next-generation sequencing technologies, which rely on short (200bp) reads, perform best when examining samples containing a single strain (monogenomic). However, these technologies provide limited resolution to the nearly 40-60% of Plasmodium falciparum samples with multiple strains (polygenomic) observed in endemic transmission settings. We aim to determine the accuracy of two sequencing technologies, short-read Illumina and long-read Oxford Nanopore technology (ONT), in successfully sequencing polygenomic strains of Plasmodium falciparum. This study focuses on characterizing the sensitivity and specificity of Illumina short-read data in detecting minority alleles in polygenomic samples. 

**Methods**

We first examined the true heterozygosity and homozygosity rates of short-read Illumina sequencing in a set of laboratory-derived DNA mixtures involving three, culture-adapted parasite strains: 3D7, Dd2, and TM90. Pairwise DNA mixtures involving 3d7/Dd2, 3d7/TM90, and Dd2/TM90 were generated at varying ratios, ranging from 1:1, 1:3, 1:5, and 1:10. The sequences for 3D7, Dd2, and TM90 were examined in silico to identify single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) where different pairwise combinations of the above strains were expected to be the same (homozygous) or different (heterozygous). This truth set was then used to quantify the true homozygosity and true heterozygosity rates observed in the empirically-mixed, polygenomic DNA mixtures.

**Results**

We observed that false heterozygous rate consistently remained at zero across all polygenomic strain ratios, while true heterozygous rates were greater than 90% for dilutions ranging from 1:1 to 1:5. True homozygous rates remained above 90% for all strain balances, however false homozygosity increased above 10% for dilutions between 1:5 to 1:10. 

**Conclusion**

Our findings show that the sensitivities and specificities for detecting true heterozygous rates depend on the relative strain proportions within polygenomic mixtures. The high false homozygous rates observed in mixtures with ratios more extreme than 1:5 indicate that Illumina short-reads will underestimate the intra-host diversity of these samples, which could be problematic when associating changes in transmission with changes in genetic diversity.
