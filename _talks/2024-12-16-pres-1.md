Accurate variant detection in *Plasmodium falciparum* is critical for understanding drug resistance and population structure. Joint calling models improve genotype accuracy by using multiple samples to impute genotypes at specific positions. However, including polygenomic samples (multiple strains in a single infection) poses challenges, as these models are optimized for monogenomic (single-strain) samples.

This study evaluates the performance of two joint callers, gGVCF and GnarlyGenotyper, on *P. falciparum* datasets. We examine how polygenomic samples impact genotyping accuracy by comparing a 1000-sample monogenomic dataset with a 3000-sample mixed dataset. By analyzing concordance and discordance between the callers, we assess the effects of polygenomic complexity on genotype calls.

We expect that joint callers designed for monogenomic samples may struggle with heterozygous site detection in polygenomic infections, potentially reducing accuracy in variant calling and relatedness estimates. Our findings will guide improvements in joint calling strategies for malaria genomics.
